Registering for the SAT is straightforward, but it has deadlines and a few rules that catch students out, such as the fact that you must register for yourself. This guide walks through the steps and lists the dates, fees and options as College Board publishes them. We read the College Board pages on November 2, 2026. Dates and fees can change, so treat this guide as a map and confirm each detail on the official pages linked at the end.

## Step by step

1. **Choose a test date.** Look at the dates and deadlines in the table below and pick one that leaves you enough time to prepare. Our [six-week SAT study plan](/guides/sat-study-plan/) shows how much time a typical plan needs.
2. **Find a test center.** College Board lets you explore test center locations before you register.
3. **Sign in to a College Board account, or create one.** The account is free. College Board says you need to register for yourself, and that a parent or counselor cannot do it for you.
4. **Complete online registration.** You enter identifying information, select a date and location, and upload a photo that meets the photo requirements.
5. **Pay the fee or use a fee waiver code.**
6. **Prepare your device and ticket.** The SAT is a digital test, so plan how you will take it and make sure you know the requirements. Our guide on [how the digital SAT works](/guides/digital-sat-format/) explains the format.

College Board says the process takes about 30 minutes, that you can start it and come back to finish later, and recommends having a photo and a form of payment ready before you begin. If you are testing in the U.S., you will also see a Waitlist option when you search for test centers; joining it costs $0. If you need to borrow a device from College Board, it says to register at least 30 days before test day.

## Fee waivers

College Board says SAT fee waivers are available to low-income 11th- and 12th-grade students in the United States or its territories, and to US citizens living abroad. You qualify if one or more of these apply:

- You are enrolled in, or eligible for, the National School Lunch Program.
- Your family income is within the USDA income eligibility guidelines.
- You are enrolled in a federal, state or local program that helps students from low-income families, such as TRIO programs like Upward Bound.
- Your family receives public assistance.
- You are unhoused, or you live in federally subsidized public housing or a foster home.
- You are a ward of the state or an orphan.

The benefits College Board lists include two free SAT tests, unlimited score reports to colleges and waived application fees at participating colleges. Ask your school counselor for a fee waiver code, or request one directly from College Board. College Board asks you to request one at least one to two weeks before the registration deadline, and says you can use a waiver once per lifetime.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until the deadline.** After the regular deadline you pay a late fee, and after the late deadline you cannot register for that date. Register early.
- **Letting someone else register for you.** College Board says you must register for yourself.
- **Uploading a photo that does not meet the rules.** Read the photo requirements before you upload.
- **Forgetting to plan for score reports.** Check the free score report window so that you do not pay for reports you could have sent for free.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a parent register me for the SAT?

No. College Board says students must register for themselves from their own College Board account. A parent or counselor cannot do it for you.

### How much does late registration cost?

College Board lists a $38 late registration fee for registering after the regular deadline but before the late registration deadline. It is free with a fee waiver.

### When should I register?

As early as you can once you have chosen a date. The regular deadline comes about two weeks before the test, and the late deadline a few days later.

### What do I need to register?

A College Board account, a photo that meets the requirements, a form of payment or a fee waiver code, and a test date and center. Having them ready before you start makes the process quicker.
