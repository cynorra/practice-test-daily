The SAT test went fully digital, and the format changed more than the screen. It's shorter than the old paper SAT, every Reading and Writing question has its own short passage, and the second half of each section adapts to how you did on the first half. Knowing exactly how the SAT exam is put together takes a lot of guesswork out of test day, and it tells you how to practice.

All of the counts and times below come from College Board's published description of the test.

## How the adaptive modules work

Each section is split into two modules. The first module of a section has a broad mix of easier, medium, and harder questions. How you do on it decides which version of the second module you get: one that leans harder or one that leans easier. College Board calls this multistage adaptive testing.

Three practical points follow from that:

- **The first module matters a lot.** It sets the difficulty of the second, so careless misses early cost more than you'd think.
- **A harder second module is usually good news.** Don't read a tough module as a sign that things are going badly.
- **You can move around inside a module, but not between modules.** Once you submit a module, you can't go back to it.

## Unscored questions

Each module includes 2 pretest questions that don't count toward your score. They look exactly like the others and aren't marked, so you can't tell which ones they are. That leaves 25 scored questions in each Reading and Writing module and 20 in each Math module. The practical lesson: never skip a question because it seems strange. It might count.

## What the Reading and Writing section covers

Every Reading and Writing question gives you a short passage, sometimes a pair of passages or a passage with a table or graph, and one question with four answer choices. The questions fall into four domains.

**Information and Ideas.** Reading for meaning: central ideas and details, using textual or numerical evidence to support a claim, and drawing logical inferences. Practice: [central ideas and details](/sat/reading-writing/central-ideas-and-details/).

**Craft and Structure.** How a text works: choosing the most precise word for the context, explaining a passage's structure or purpose, and connecting two related texts. Practice: [words in context](/sat/reading-writing/words-in-context/).

**Expression of Ideas.** Revising writing for its goal: picking the transition that fits the logic, and synthesizing notes to meet a stated purpose. Practice: [transitions](/sat/reading-writing/transitions/) and [rhetorical synthesis](/sat/reading-writing/rhetorical-synthesis/).

**Standard English Conventions.** Grammar and punctuation: sentence boundaries (commas, semicolons, colons, dashes) and form, structure, and sense (agreement, verb tense, pronouns, modifiers). Practice: [boundaries](/sat/reading-writing/boundaries/) and [form, structure, and sense](/sat/reading-writing/form-structure-and-sense/).

## What the Math section covers

You can use a calculator for the whole Math section, including the Desmos graphing calculator built into the testing app, and a reference sheet of common formulas is available. Most questions are multiple choice with four options; the rest are student-produced responses, where you type in your answer. The questions fall into four domains.

**Algebra.** Linear equations and inequalities, linear functions, and systems of linear equations. Practice: [linear equations](/sat/math/linear-equations-one-variable/), [linear functions](/sat/math/linear-functions/), [systems](/sat/math/systems-of-linear-equations/), [inequalities](/sat/math/linear-inequalities/).

**Advanced Math.** Equivalent expressions, nonlinear equations such as quadratics, and nonlinear functions such as exponential models. Practice: [quadratic equations](/sat/math/quadratic-equations/), [exponents](/sat/math/exponents-and-equivalent-expressions/), [nonlinear functions](/sat/math/nonlinear-functions/).

**Problem-Solving and Data Analysis.** Ratios, rates, and units; percentages; reading and summarizing data; and probability. Practice: [percentages](/sat/math/percentages/), [ratios and rates](/sat/math/ratios-rates-and-units/), [mean and median](/sat/math/one-variable-data/), [probability](/sat/math/probability/).

**Geometry and Trigonometry.** Area and volume, lines, angles, and triangles, right-triangle trigonometry, and circles. Practice: [right triangles](/sat/math/right-triangles-and-trigonometry/), [circles](/sat/math/circles/), [area and volume](/sat/math/area-and-volume/).

## Pacing each module

The averages in the table are a starting point, not a rule for every question. Short convention questions in Reading and Writing usually take well under a minute, which buys time for longer inference and synthesis questions. In Math, quick one-step questions buy time for multistep word problems.

A checkpoint halfway through each module is easier to manage than a per-question timer:

- **Reading and Writing module (32 minutes, 27 questions):** about 14 questions done with 16 minutes left.
- **Math module (35 minutes, 22 questions):** about 11 questions done with 17 to 18 minutes left.

If you reach the checkpoint behind, make faster decisions on questions you're unsure of, flag them, and come back if you have time. There's no penalty for wrong answers, so answer everything before a module ends.

## How to practice for this format

The format rewards two kinds of practice. Full-length, timed tests build pacing and stamina; the [guide to taking an SAT practice test](/guides/sat-practice-test/) covers how to run one at home. Skill-by-skill practice fixes the specific question types you miss. Because every Reading and Writing question is a short, self-contained task, it's very practical to practice one skill at a time, then mix skills together closer to test day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions are on the SAT?

The Digital SAT has 98 questions: 54 in Reading and Writing and 44 in Math. Each module includes 2 unscored pretest questions that look like the rest.

### How long is the SAT?

Testing time is about 2 hours and 14 minutes: 64 minutes for Reading and Writing and 70 minutes for Math, with a short break between the two sections.

### Is the Digital SAT adaptive?

Yes, by module. Your performance on the first module of each section decides whether the second module leans harder or easier. Within a module, the questions don't change based on your answers.

### Can I use a calculator on the whole Math section?

Yes. A calculator is allowed on every Math question, and the testing app includes the Desmos graphing calculator.

### What is a good way to start preparing for the SAT exam?

Take one full-length practice test to find your starting point, list the skills you missed, and practice those skills one at a time before taking another full test.
