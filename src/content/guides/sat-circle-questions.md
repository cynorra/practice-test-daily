<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Most SAT circle questions use one idea: a central angle takes the same fraction of the circle's circumference (for arc length) and of its area (for sector area) as it does of 360°. For circle equations, (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² has center (h, k) and radius r, so watch the signs and take the square root of the right side.</p></div>

Circles are a regular part of the Geometry and Trigonometry domain on SAT Math. The test questions range from a one-step area problem to finding the center of a circle from an equation you have to rearrange first. The good news is that nearly all of them rely on a few relationships, and the reference sheet available during the test lists the basic formulas.

This guide covers each type of SAT circle question with a worked example. Every answer was checked by code.

## The formulas you need

- **Circumference:** C = 2πr (or πd)
- **Area:** A = πr²
- **Full circle:** 360°, or 2π radians
- **Arc length:** (central angle ÷ 360°) × 2πr
- **Sector area:** (central angle ÷ 360°) × πr²
- **Circle equation:** (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r², with center (h, k) and radius r

## Area and circumference

**Example 1.** A circle has a circumference of 22π. What is its area?

From C = 2πr: 2πr = 22π, so r = 11. Then A = π(11)² = **121π**.

The trap answer is 484π, which uses 22 as the radius. Whenever a question gives you circumference or diameter, find the radius first and write it down.

## Arc length

An arc is a piece of the circumference. Its share of the circle matches its central angle's share of 360°.

**Example 2.** A circle has a radius of 8. What is the length of an arc formed by a central angle of 120°?

Circumference: 2π(8) = 16π. A 120° angle is 120/360 = 1/3 of the circle. Arc length = (1/3) × 16π = **16π/3**.

**Example 3.** A circle has a radius of 4. What is the length of an arc formed by a central angle of 90°?

Circumference: 8π. 90° is 1/4 of the circle, so the arc length is **2π**.

A common trap answer for arc questions uses the area (πr²) instead of the circumference. Arc length is a length, so it comes from the circumference.

## Sector area

A sector is a "pizza slice" of the circle. Its share of the area matches its central angle's share of 360°.

**Example 4.** A circle has a radius of 6. What is the area of a sector with a central angle of 60°?

Full area: π(6)² = 36π. 60° is 60/360 = 1/6 of the circle. Sector area = (1/6) × 36π = **6π**.

## Angles in radians

The SAT sometimes gives angles in radians. Two facts cover almost everything:

- **180° = π radians**, so multiply degrees by π/180 to convert to radians, or radians by 180/π to convert to degrees.
- **Arc length = rθ** when θ is in radians.

**Example 5.** A circle has a radius of 5. What is the length of an arc with a central angle of 2π/5 radians?

Arc length = rθ = 5 × 2π/5 = **2π**. (In degrees, 2π/5 radians is 72°, and 72/360 × 10π gives the same 2π.)

## Inscribed angles and tangent lines

- **Inscribed angle:** an angle with its vertex on the circle is half of the central angle that intercepts the same arc. If an inscribed angle measures 40°, the central angle on the same arc measures 80°.
- **Tangent line:** a tangent touches the circle at one point and is perpendicular to the radius at that point. This often creates a right triangle, which lets you use the Pythagorean theorem.

## Circle equations

**Example 6.** The equation of a circle is (x + 6)² + (y − 1)² = 64. What are its center and radius?

Match it to (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². Since x + 6 = x − (−6), h = −6. And y − 1 means k = 1. The center is **(−6, 1)**, and since r² = 64, the radius is **8**.

Two traps appear in almost every circle equation question: flipping the signs of the center to (6, −1), and giving 64 as the radius instead of its square root.

**Example 7: completing the square.** What are the center and radius of the circle x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0?

Group the x terms and the y terms, and move the constant: (x² − 6x) + (y² + 4y) = 12.

Complete the square for each: take half of each linear coefficient and square it. Half of −6 is −3, and (−3)² = 9. Half of 4 is 2, and 2² = 4. Add both to each side:

(x² − 6x + 9) + (y² + 4y + 4) = 12 + 9 + 4

(x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25

The center is **(3, −2)** and the radius is **5**. Forgetting to add the 9 and the 4 to the right side is the most common error here.

## Common mistakes on circle questions

- **Using the diameter as the radius.** Always convert to r first.
- **Mixing up arc length and sector area.** Length uses 2πr; area uses πr².
- **Sign errors in the center.** (x + 6) means h = −6.
- **Forgetting to take the square root** of r² in a circle equation.
- **Not balancing both sides** when completing the square.

## Frequently asked questions

### What circle formulas are on the SAT reference sheet?

The reference sheet includes the circumference and area of a circle and the fact that a circle has 360 degrees, or 2π radians. Arc length, sector area, and the circle equation are worth knowing on your own.

### How do you find arc length on the SAT?

Multiply the circumference by the central angle's fraction of the circle: (angle ÷ 360°) × 2πr. In radians, arc length is simply r times the angle.

### How do you find the center and radius from a circle equation?

Write it as (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². The center is (h, k), with the signs flipped from what appears inside the parentheses, and the radius is the square root of the right side. If the equation isn't in that form, complete the square first.

### What is the difference between arc length and sector area?

Arc length is part of the circle's edge, measured from the circumference. Sector area is the region inside the slice, measured from the area.

### Are circle questions hard on the SAT?

Most are short once you know the fraction-of-the-circle idea and the circle equation. The harder ones add a step, such as completing the square or converting radians, but they use the same relationships.
