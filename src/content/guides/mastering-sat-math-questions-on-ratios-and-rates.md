<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Almost every SAT ratio, rate and unit-conversion question reduces to one of three moves: add the ratio parts to find the whole, multiply rate by time, or write each conversion as a fraction so the unwanted unit cancels. The classic trap is converting a square or cubic unit with the linear factor instead of squaring or cubing it.</div>

<figure class="guide-figure"><img src="/images/guides/topic-ratios-rates-and-units-figure.png" alt="A bar split 3 to 5 representing 40 total: the 3 parts equal 15 and the 5 parts equal 25, showing that the ratio 3 to 5 has 8 parts in all." width="1000" height="560" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Add the ratio parts to get the whole, then divide to find the size of one part.</figcaption></figure>

<p>Ratio, rate and unit questions sit in the Problem-Solving and Data Analysis domain of the Digital SAT Math section. They are short and use simple numbers, which is exactly why students lose points on them: they rush, and the wrong answer choices are built from the most natural mistakes. This guide gives you the three moves, three fully worked questions, and a quick check to run before you lock in an answer.</p>

<h2>Move 1: Ratios are parts of a whole</h2>

<p>A ratio such as <code>3 : 5</code> does not tell you the actual amounts. It tells you how many equal parts each group has. To find real amounts, add the parts, divide the total by that sum to get the size of one part, then multiply.</p>

<p>For the bar in the figure above, a total of <code>40</code> split in the ratio <code>3 : 5</code> has <code>3 + 5 = 8</code> parts. One part is <code>40 ÷ 8 = 5</code>, so the groups are <code>3 × 5 = 15</code> and <code>5 × 5 = 25</code>. Check: <code>15 + 25 = 40</code>.</p>

<h2>Move 2: Rate times time</h2>

<p>A rate is a ratio with different units on top and bottom, such as miles per hour or pages per minute. The core relationship is <code>distance = rate × time</code>, and the same pattern works for work: <code>amount = rate × time</code>.</p>

<p>A train moving at <code>25</code> miles per hour for <code>6</code> hours covers <code>25 × 6 = 150</code> miles. A printer that prints <code>45</code> pages in <code>3</code> minutes works at <code>45 ÷ 3 = 15</code> pages per minute, so in <code>20</code> minutes it prints <code>15 × 20 = 300</code> pages. Always find the unit rate first, because it turns every later step into a single multiplication.</p>

<h2>Move 3: Unit conversion as fractions that cancel</h2>

<p>Write each conversion as a fraction equal to <code>1</code>, for example <code>3 ft / 1 yd</code>, and arrange it so the unit you want to remove sits in the opposite position from where it appears in your starting quantity. If the units cancel to what the question asks for, the setup is right.</p>

<p>The square-unit row is where most points are lost: one yard is <code>3</code> feet, but one square yard is <code>3 × 3 = 9</code> square feet.</p>

<h2>Three worked questions</h2>

<h3>Question 1: Ratio of parts</h3>

<p>The ratio of red marbles to blue marbles in a bag is <code>1</code> to <code>7</code>. If the bag holds <code>32</code> marbles in total, how many are red?</p>

<ul><li>A) <code>32</code></li><li>B) <code>4</code></li><li>C) <code>25</code></li><li>D) <code>28</code></li></ul>

<p><strong>Solution:</strong> The ratio has <code>1 + 7 = 8</code> parts. One part is <code>32 ÷ 8 = 4</code> marbles. Red is <code>1</code> part, so there are <code>4</code> red marbles. The answer is B.</p>

<p><strong>Why the others tempt:</strong> Choice D (<code>28</code>) is the number of blue marbles, <code>7 × 4</code>. Choice A (<code>32</code>) is the total. Choice C (<code>25</code>) comes from <code>32 − 7</code>, subtracting the ratio number instead of using parts. <strong>Check:</strong> <code>4 + 28 = 32</code>.</p>

<h3>Question 2: Square units</h3>

<p>A square rug measures <code>6</code> feet on each side. What is its area in square yards?</p>

<p><strong>Solution:</strong> Convert the side first: <code>6 ft ÷ 3 = 2 yd</code>. The area is <code>2 × 2 = 4</code> square yards. As a cross-check, the area in square feet is <code>6 × 6 = 36</code>, and <code>36 ÷ 9 = 4</code>.</p>

<p><strong>Why students miss it:</strong> Many students compute <code>36</code> square feet and divide by <code>3</code>, getting <code>12</code>. That treats a square unit like a linear unit. <strong>Check:</strong> a <code>2 yd × 2 yd</code> rug is small, so <code>4</code> is reasonable and <code>12</code> is not.</p>

<h3>Question 3: Converting a rate</h3>

<p>A car travels at <code>60</code> miles per hour. How many feet per second is that?</p>

<p><strong>Solution:</strong> Set up the cancellation: <code>60 mi/hr × 5,280 ft/mi × 1 hr/3,600 s</code>. The <code>mi</code> and <code>hr</code> cancel, leaving feet per second. Multiply: <code>60 × 5,280 = 316,800</code>, and <code>316,800 ÷ 3,600 = 88</code>. The car travels <code>88</code> feet per second.</p>

<p><strong>Why students miss it:</strong> They convert only one unit, for example miles to feet, and forget that hours must also become seconds. <strong>Check:</strong> one mile per minute is <code>60</code> miles per hour and <code>88</code> feet per second, so <code>88</code> is a sensible size.</p>

<h2>A 20-second check before you answer</h2>

<ol><li>Did the units on your answer match the units in the question? If you were asked for yards and your answer is in feet, stop.</li><li>For a ratio, do the parts add back to the total?</li><li>For a square or cubic unit, did you convert with the factor squared or cubed (<code>3²= 9</code>, <code>3³ = 27</code>)?</li><li>Is the answer a sensible size? A rug larger than a house means a step went wrong.</li></ol>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>How do you find the whole when you only know one part of a ratio?</h3>

<p>Divide the known amount by its share of the ratio to get one part, then multiply by the sum of all parts. If red is <code>1</code> part and equals <code>4</code> marbles, one part is <code>4</code>, and a <code>1 : 7</code> ratio has <code>8</code> parts, so the whole is <code>32</code>.</p>

<h3>How do you convert square feet to square yards?</h3>

<p>Divide by <code>9</code>, not <code>3</code>. One yard is <code>3</code> feet, so one square yard is <code>3 × 3 = 9</code> square feet. A rug of <code>36</code> square feet is therefore <code>4</code> square yards.</p>

<h3>How many seconds are in an hour for SAT unit conversions?</h3>

<p>There are <code>3,600</code> seconds in an hour, from <code>60 × 60</code>. This number appears in miles-per-hour to feet-per-second conversions, where <code>60</code> mi/hr equals <code>88</code> ft/s.</p>

<h3>Where do ratio and rate questions appear on the SAT?</h3>

<p>They are part of the Problem-Solving and Data Analysis domain of the Math section, which has <code>44</code> questions across two modules of <code>22</code>, each module lasting <code>35</code> minutes.</p>
