<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> The digital SAT has two sections (Reading and Writing, and Math), 98 questions and about 2 hours 14 minutes of testing, and it is scored from 400 to 1600. The ACT has English, Math and Reading as required tests, with Science and Writing as optional add-ons, and its composite score is reported on a 1 to 36 scale. Colleges that accept both treat them as equal, so the better choice is the test on which you score higher in practice.</p></div>

Most US colleges accept either the SAT or the ACT, so the real question is which one suits you. The two tests cover similar skills, but they ask for them in different ways: the SAT gives you fewer questions and more time per question, while the ACT is faster and has separate English, Math and Reading tests. This guide compares the structure of each test and gives a simple way to choose.

ACT says on that page that the Science and Writing sections are optional add-ons and do not affect the composite score. If you take only the three required tests, the section times listed above add up to 125 minutes (35 + 50 + 40); adding Science brings it to 165, and adding Writing as well to 205. ACT's page does not state a total time itself, so those totals are our own addition of the published section times.

## What feels different

**Time pressure.** The SAT's 98 questions in about 134 minutes leave roughly 80 seconds per question. The ACT's three required tests have 131 questions in 125 minutes, which is closer to 57 seconds per question. If you tend to run out of time, the SAT is gentler on pacing; if you work quickly and like to move on, the ACT may feel natural.

**Reading.** The SAT's Reading and Writing section uses short passages with one question each. The ACT's Reading test is a separate 40-minute test with 36 questions, so you read for a longer stretch before you switch to something else.

**Science.** The ACT's Science test is optional, according to ACT. The SAT has no separate science section, though some Math and Reading questions use charts and tables.

**Calculators.** According to ACT's overview, a calculator is for the math test only. Our [SAT practice test guide](/guides/sat-practice-test/) explains what is allowed on the digital SAT.

## How to choose

1. **Take one full, timed practice test of each**, on separate days, in the same conditions.
2. **Convert the results** with the official ACT/SAT concordance table, which puts both scores on one scale, and compare them.
3. **Look at how you felt**, not just the number: which test did you finish with time to spare, and which did you find more frustrating?
4. **Check your colleges.** Most accept both, but check each school's policy, especially for scholarships and for programs that ask for particular sections.
5. **Commit to one.** Splitting your prep between two tests slows your progress on both.

## When the SAT may suit you better

- You prefer more time per question.
- You like short passages and a smaller number of questions.
- You do best when the test adapts and does not ask you to rush through easy items.

## When the ACT may suit you better

- You work fast and like a steady pace.
- You like the option of adding a separate Science test.
- You find the ACT English test's grammar questions easier than the SAT's mixture of skills.

## Preparing for either one

Whichever you choose, the method is the same: find your weak skills with a full practice test, practice those skills, and retest. If you pick the SAT, our [six-week SAT study plan](/guides/sat-study-plan/) is a good starting schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do colleges prefer the SAT or the ACT?

Most colleges that accept both treat them equally and have no preference. Check the admissions page of each school you are applying to, because policies on test requirements and on sections such as ACT Writing differ.

### Can I take both the SAT and the ACT?

Yes, and many students do. A better plan for most students is to take one full practice test of each, then choose the test where you score higher and spend your prep time on it.

### Is the ACT Science section really optional?

According to ACT's overview page, Science and Writing are optional add-ons that do not affect the composite score. Some colleges may still ask for them, so check each school's requirements.

### Which test has more questions?

The three required ACT tests have 131 questions in total (50 English, 45 Math and 36 Reading), compared with 98 on the digital SAT, though the ACT also has 40 optional Science questions and one optional Writing prompt.
