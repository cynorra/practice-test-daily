<h2>Introduction to SAT Trigonometry and Geometry</h2><p>Preparing for sat test questions in the geometry and trigonometry domain requires a systematic approach. On test day, you will encounter problems involving right triangles, angle measures, and side lengths. By following a strict decision procedure, you can eliminate careless errors and work through each problem efficiently.</p><p>The digital SAT math section consists of 44 questions divided into two modules of 22 questions each, giving you 35 minutes per module. Within these modules, you will face algebra, advanced math, problem-solving and data analysis, and geometry and trigonometry. Knowing how to methodically evaluate right triangles is essential for securing points in the math domain.</p><h2>The Test-Day Decision Procedure for Right Triangles</h2><p>When you see a triangle diagram or a text description of a triangle on your screen, execute this exact 4-step sequence to determine your next move.</p><ol><li><strong>Is the triangle explicitly stated or proven to be a right triangle?</strong> If yes, proceed to step 2. If no, look for perpendicular lines or coordinate geometry clues to establish a right angle.</li><li><strong>Which angle are you asked to evaluate?</strong> Locate the target acute angle and designate it as your reference angle, such as angle <code>A</code>.</li><li><strong>Which sides are given, and which side is unknown?</strong> Label the hypotenuse <code>c</code> as the side opposite the right angle. Then label the opposite side and the adjacent side relative to your reference angle <code>A</code>.</li><li><strong>Should you use trigonometric ratios or the Pythagorean theorem?</strong> If side lengths and an angle relationship are involved, apply trigonometric definitions. If only side lengths are involved, apply the Pythagorean theorem <code>a^2 + b^2 = c^2</code>.</li></ul><h2>Applying the Procedure: Practice Problem 1</h2><p>Let us apply our decision procedure to our first official practice item from the <a href="/sat/math/right-triangles-and-trigonometry/">right triangles and trigonometry</a> problem bank.</p><p><strong>Problem:</strong> In right triangle ABC, angle C is the right angle, BC = 7, AC = 24 and AB = 25. What is the value of <code>tan(A)</code>?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>7/25</code> | <code>24/7</code> | <code>24/25</code> | <code>7/24</code></p><p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong> Following our procedure, we verify that angle C is the right angle, making side <code>AB = 25</code> the hypotenuse. For reference angle <code>A</code>, the side opposite is <code>BC = 7</code> and the adjacent side is <code>AC = 24</code>. The tangent ratio is defined as opposite over adjacent. Therefore, <code>tan(A) = 7/24</code>. The correct choice is <code>7/24</code>.</p><h2>Applying the Procedure: Practice Problem 2</h2><p>Let us test our procedure on a second problem from our <a href="/sat/math/right-triangles-and-trigonometry/">right triangles and trigonometry</a> collection.</p><p><strong>Problem:</strong> In right triangle ABC, angle C is the right angle, BC = 3, AC = 4 and AB = 5. What is the value of <code>tan(A)</code>?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>3/5</code> | <code>4/3</code> | <code>4/5</code> | <code>3/4</code></p><p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong> Angle C is the right angle, so <code>AB = 5</code> is the hypotenuse. For angle <code>A</code>, the opposite side is <code>BC = 3</code> and the adjacent side is <code>AC = 4</code>. Applying the tangent ratio <code>opposite/adjacent</code> gives us <code>3/4</code>. The correct choice is <code>3/4</code>.</p><h2>Applying the Procedure: Practice Problem 3</h2><p>Let us examine a third scenario involving missing side lengths rather than trigonometric ratios, found in our <a href="/sat/math/right-triangles-and-trigonometry/">right triangles and trigonometry</a> practice set.</p><p><strong>Problem:</strong> A right triangle has a hypotenuse of length 29 and one leg of length 20. What is the length of the other leg?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>49</code> | <code>20</code> | <code>21</code> | <code>9</code></p><p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong> Following our decision procedure, we see that we are given the hypotenuse <code>c = 29</code> and one leg <code>a = 20</code>, and we need to find the other leg <code>b</code>. We use the Pythagorean theorem: <code>a^2 + b^2 = c^2</code>. Substituting our values gives <code>20^2 + b^2 = 29^2</code>, which simplifies to <code>400 + b^2 = 841</code>. Subtracting <code>400</code> from both sides yields <code>b^2 = 441</code>. Taking the square root gives <code>b = 21</code>. The correct choice is <code>21</code>.</p><h2>Summary of Trigonometric Ratios</h2><p>To ensure you handle any related sat test questions with confidence, review the fundamental definitions in the table below.</p><table><thead><tr><th>Ratio</th><th>Abbreviation</th><th>Definition</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Sine</td><td>sin</td><td><code>opposite / hypotenuse</code></td></tr><tr><td>Cosine</td><td>cos</td><td><code>adjacent / hypotenuse</code></td></tr><tr><td>Tangent</td><td>tan</td><td><code>opposite / adjacent</code></td></tr></tbody></table><p>For more practice across all sections, review the <a href="/guides/digital-sat-format/">Digital SAT Format: Sections, Modules, Timing and Question Counts</a> guide, or explore general practice strategies at <a href="/guides/sat-practice-daily-skill-prep-for-math-reading-writing/">SAT Practice: Daily Skill Prep for Math, Reading and Writing</a>.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>How many sat test questions are there in total across the entire exam?</h3>
<p>The digital SAT contains a total of 98 scored and unscored questions across two sections. Reading and Writing has 54 questions across two modules, while Math has 44 questions across two modules, resulting in a total testing time of 134 minutes.</p>
<h3>How should I practice sat math questions for geometry and trigonometry?</h3>
<p>You should practice by working through specific problem types and mastering core formulas like the Pythagorean theorem <code>a^2 + b^2 = c^2</code> and trigonometric ratios across the 22 questions in each Math module.</p>
<h3>What is the scoring scale for the digital SAT?</h3>
<p>The total score ranges from 400 to 1600. Each of the two main sections, Reading and Writing and Math, is scored separately on a scale from 200 to 800.</p>
