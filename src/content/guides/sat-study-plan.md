<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> A strong SAT study plan has three parts: a full practice test to find your weak skills, focused skill practice on those weaknesses most days of the week, and another full test every week or two to measure progress. Six weeks of about five 45- to 60-minute sessions per week is a realistic schedule for most students.</p></div>

You don't need an expensive course to prepare well for the SAT. You need a plan that tells you what to do today, a way to find your weak spots, and enough practice on those spots to fix them. This SAT study plan lays out six weeks of SAT prep at home using free tools, with a clear goal for each week.

Adjust the length to your situation. If your test is three months away, stretch each phase. If it's three weeks away, compress the plan and focus on your top three weak skills.

## Before you start: your free toolkit

- **Full-length practice tests:** College Board's Bluebook app has free, full-length adaptive practice tests that run like the real Digital SAT.
- **Skill practice with explanations:** the [SAT Math](/sat/math/) and [Reading and Writing](/sat/reading-writing/) practice pages on this site cover each skill with a short lesson and question sets that explain every answer choice.
- **Free courses:** Khan Academy offers free official Digital SAT prep, made with College Board, if you'd like video lessons.
- **An error log:** a notebook or spreadsheet with four columns: question, skill, why you missed it, and the fix.

## Week 1: Find your starting point

**Goal: know your score and your weak skills.**

1. Take a full practice test in one sitting with real timing. The [SAT practice test guide](/guides/sat-practice-test/) covers how to set it up.
2. The next day, review every question you missed or guessed on. Log the skill and the reason for each one.
3. Count your misses by skill. Pick the three to five skills that cost you the most.
4. Read the [Digital SAT format guide](/guides/digital-sat-format/) so you know exactly what each module contains.

By the end of week 1 you should have a short, specific list, such as: "percent change, systems of equations, comma splices, transitions."

## Weeks 2 and 3: Fix the biggest weaknesses

**Goal: turn your top weak skills into strengths.**

Five sessions a week, 45 to 60 minutes each. A session looks like this:

1. **Ten minutes on the rule.** Read the lesson for one skill, such as the [percentages lesson](/sat/math/percentages/) or the [SAT grammar rules guide](/guides/sat-grammar-rules/).
2. **Thirty minutes of practice on that skill.** Work one set slowly, read every explanation, then do a second set faster.
3. **Ten minutes on your error log.** Add new misses and reread old ones.

Alternate Math and Reading and Writing days so both stay fresh. Spend roughly two sessions on each of your top skills before moving on. Many students get the most out of the first few skills, because those were costing them the most points.

## Week 4: Second practice test and adjust

**Goal: measure progress and update the plan.**

Take a second full practice test early in the week, then review it the same way as the first. Compare the two error logs:

- Skills that disappeared from your misses: keep them warm with one short set a week.
- Skills still showing up: keep them on the list, and look at *why* you're still missing them. Is it the rule, or misreading the question?
- New skills that appeared: add them to your list for weeks 5 and 6.

Don't worry if the total score barely moved. Scores bounce between practice tests. A shrinking error log is the more reliable sign that you're improving.

## Week 5: Mixed practice under time pressure

**Goal: use your skills quickly and in any order, the way the test presents them.**

Switch from single-skill sets to mixed, timed practice. On the real test you get 32 minutes for each Reading and Writing module of 27 questions and 35 minutes for each Math module of 22 questions, so practice at that pace:

- Mix two or three skills per session and set a timer.
- Practice the halfway checkpoint: about 14 Reading and Writing questions with 16 minutes left, or 11 Math questions with 17 to 18 minutes left.
- Try the [12 mixed SAT math questions](/guides/sat-math-practice-questions/) and the [SAT reading practice questions guide](/guides/sat-reading-practice-questions/) as timed drills.

## Week 6: Final test and test-day readiness

**Goal: rehearse test day and stay sharp without burning out.**

1. Take a final full practice test early in the week at the same time of day as your real test.
2. Review it, then spend the next two or three sessions only on what it showed.
3. Ease off in the last two days: a short review of your error log, nothing new.
4. Get your test-day logistics ready: device charged and checked in Bluebook, admission ticket, ID, and a plan for getting to the test center.

## How much time should you spend on SAT prep?

There's no single right number. It depends on your starting point, your goal, and how much time you have. What matters more than total hours is how the hours are used: focused practice on weak skills, with every miss reviewed, beats long sessions of random questions. If you can only manage three sessions a week, keep the same order and stretch the plan to eight or nine weeks.

## Mistakes that waste SAT study time

- **Only taking full tests.** They measure; they don't teach. Most of your time should go to skill practice.
- **Skipping the review.** A practice test you don't review gives you a number and little else.
- **Practicing what you're already good at.** It feels productive, but it doesn't move your score much.
- **Not reading explanations for correct answers.** If you guessed right, you still don't know the skill.
- **Cramming the night before.** Rest and a calm morning help more than one more set.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I study for the SAT?

Many students do well with six to ten weeks of steady practice, about five sessions a week. Start earlier if you're aiming for a big improvement or can only study a few times a week.

### Can I prepare for the SAT at home for free?

Yes. Bluebook's full-length practice tests, Khan Academy's official SAT prep, and the skill practice on this site are all free.

### What should I study first for the SAT?

Take a full practice test first, then start with the skills that cost you the most points. For many students those are a few Algebra and Problem-Solving skills in Math and sentence-boundary punctuation in Reading and Writing.

### How many practice tests should I take before the SAT?

Three or four full practice tests spread across your study plan is plenty for most students, as long as you review each one carefully.

### Is a study plan better than an SAT prep course?

A course can help if you want structure and live teaching. But a good plan, free practice tests, and focused skill practice cover the same ground, and what you do with each practice test matters more than where the material comes from.
