<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> After every SAT mock test, log each missed or guessed question with four things: the skill, the reason you missed it, the correct reasoning, and the fix. Group the misses by skill and by reason. The two or three biggest groups are what you practice until the next test.</p></div>

A mock test gives you a score, but the score is the least useful thing it produces. The useful part is the list of questions you got wrong and *why*. Students who review each SAT practice exam carefully tend to improve steadily; students who take test after test without reviewing often see their scores stall, because they keep making the same mistakes.

This guide shows how to review an SAT mock test in about an hour, using a simple error log, and how to turn that review into a practice plan.

## When to review

Review the same day or the next day, while you still remember your thinking. Your reasoning on a question is often more revealing than the question itself, and you'll forget it within a couple of days.

Set aside about an hour. Review the Reading and Writing section and the Math section separately if you need to split it up.

## Which questions to review

- **Every question you got wrong.**
- **Every question you guessed on, even if you got it right.** A lucky guess hides a skill you don't have yet.
- **Any question that took far longer than it should have.** On the real test, Reading and Writing averages about 71 seconds per question and Math about 95 seconds. A question that took three minutes cost you time somewhere else.

## The four-column error log

Use a notebook or a spreadsheet with these columns:

Keep the "Fix" column short and specific: one sentence you could act on in the next question of that type.

## The five reasons students miss SAT questions

Every miss fits one of these. Label each one honestly, because each reason has a different fix.

**1. Didn't know the content.** You didn't know the rule or the formula. *Fix:* learn it, then practice that skill with a full set. This is the easiest kind of miss to fix.

**2. Fell for a trap answer.** You knew the topic but chose a choice designed to catch a common mistake, such as adding two percent changes or using the wrong denominator in probability. *Fix:* study the explanation for the trap, not just the right answer. Traps repeat.

**3. Misread the question.** You solved for x when it asked for x + y, or chose a detail when it asked for the main idea. *Fix:* underline or restate the question before you start.

**4. Careless slip.** A sign error, an arithmetic mistake, or a skipped word. *Fix:* check answers by plugging them back in, and slow down slightly on the first module, which sets the difficulty of the second.

**5. Ran out of time.** You never reached the question, or rushed it. *Fix:* practice the halfway checkpoint in every module, and stop rereading passages you've already understood.

## A worked review

Here are two misses from a practice set and how they'd be logged.

**Miss 1.** *A store raises the price of a jacket by 10%. Later, it lowers the new price by 25%. What is the overall change?* The student chose "Decrease of 15%." The correct answer is a decrease of 17.5%, because the 25% applies to the higher price: 1.10 × 0.75 = 0.825.

- **Skill:** percentages
- **Reason:** trap answer (added the percents)
- **Fix:** convert each change to a multiplier and multiply

**Miss 2.** *The list of required supplies ___ on the course website.* The student chose "appear." The correct answer is "appears," because the subject is "list," not "supplies."

- **Skill:** subject-verb agreement
- **Reason:** didn't apply the rule (matched the nearest noun)
- **Fix:** cross out the phrase between the subject and the verb

Both misses now point to specific practice: the [percentages set](/sat/math/percentages/) and the [form, structure, and sense set](/sat/reading-writing/form-structure-and-sense/). The full explanations of these traps are in the [SAT percentage problems guide](/guides/sat-percentage-problems/) and the [SAT grammar rules guide](/guides/sat-grammar-rules/).

## Turn the review into a plan

After logging every miss, count:

- **By skill:** which skills appear most often?
- **By reason:** are most misses content, traps, misreading, slips, or time?

Then plan the next week or two around the biggest groups. If most misses are content, practice those skills one at a time. If most are misreading or slips, do timed mixed sets and focus on the habit (underlining the question, checking answers) rather than on new content. The [6-week SAT study plan](/guides/sat-study-plan/) shows how to build that into a weekly schedule.

## Review your log before every test

Your error log is the best study guide you'll have, because it's built from your own mistakes. Reread the "Fix" column before each new mock test and again a day or two before the real SAT. Most students find the same few fixes come up again and again, and seeing them in one place makes them stick.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should an SAT mock test review take?

About an hour for a full test is typical. It takes longer the first time you do it and gets faster as your error log grows.

### Should I review questions I got right?

Review the ones you guessed on or spent too long on. A correct guess means you don't yet have that skill reliably, and a slow correct answer costs time elsewhere.

### How often should I take an SAT mock test?

Every week or two works well for most students, with skill practice in between. Taking tests more often leaves too little time to fix what each one shows.

### What is an SAT error log?

It's a list of every question you missed or guessed on, with the skill tested, why you missed it, the correct reasoning, and a short fix. Grouping the entries shows exactly what to practice.

### Where can I take a free SAT mock test online?

College Board's Bluebook app has free full-length practice tests that run like the real Digital SAT. Skill practice with explanations is free on this site. The [SAT practice test guide](/guides/sat-practice-test/) explains how to take a full test at home.
