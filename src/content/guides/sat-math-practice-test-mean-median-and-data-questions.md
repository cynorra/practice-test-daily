A standard strategy for finding a missing value is setting the target total equal to <code>n &times; mean</code> and subtracting the known sum.</p></div>

<h2>Mastering Data Analysis on Your SAT Math Practice Test</h2><p>Solving data analysis items on a modern <strong>sat math practice test</strong> requires precision with core statistical concepts like mean, median, and missing values. The digital SAT features 44 total math questions divided into two 35-minute modules. Within the Problem-Solving and Data Analysis domain, statistical measures appear frequently. Avoiding common calculation traps is key to earning a strong score in this section.</p><h2>The Unsorted-List Trap: Wrong Attempt vs. Correct Approach</h2><p>Many test takers make fast errors on median questions by selecting the middle term of the raw list without ordering it first.</p><p><strong>Wrong attempt:</strong></p><p>Consider the data set: <code>28, 18, 12, 3, 6, 23, 14</code>.</p><p>A student looks at the unsorted list <code>28, 18, 12, 3, 6, 23, 14</code>, picks the 4th number as it appears, and answers <code>3</code>.</p><p><strong>What is wrong with it:</strong> The median is defined as the middle value only after the data set has been arranged in ascending or descending numerical order. Picking the middle element from an unsorted list selects an arbitrary number rather than the true median.</p><p><strong>Correct version:</strong></p><p>Step 1: Sort the <code>7</code> values from least to greatest: <code>3, 6, 12, 14, 18, 23, 28</code>.</p><p>Step 2: Identify the middle position using <code>(n + 1) / 2</code>, which gives <code>(7 + 1) / 2 = 4</code>. The 4th number in the ordered list is <code>14</code>.</p><p>You can try more questions like this on our <a href="/sat/math/one-variable-data/">one-variable data practice page</a>.</p><h2>Mean and Missing-Value Problems Explained</h2><p>Another frequent item type asks you to find a missing number given the mean of a group. You can review overall test timing in our guide to the <a href="/guides/digital-sat-format/">Digital SAT Format</a>.</p><table><thead><tr><th>Statistical Measure</th><th>Formula / Definition</th><th>Common Trap</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Mean</td><td><code>Sum of values / Number of values</code></td><td>Dividing by the wrong total count or miscounting items</td></tr><tr><td>Median</td><td>Middle value of sorted data</td><td>Selecting the middle number from an unsorted list</td></tr><tr><td>Missing Value</td><td><code>Target Sum - Known Sum</code></td><td>Adding the mean directly to the known numbers</td></tr></tbody></table><h3>Practice Problem 1: Unsorted List Median</h3><p><strong>Question:</strong> What is the median of the data set <code>28, 18, 12, 3, 6, 23, 14</code>?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>15</code> | <code>13</code> | <code>3</code> | <code>14</code></p><p><strong>Correct Answer:</strong> <code>14</code></p><p><strong>Wrong attempt:</strong></p><p>Selecting <code>3</code> because it sits in the middle position of <code>28, 18, 12, 3, 6, 23, 14</code>.</p><p><strong>What is wrong:</strong> The list was not sorted before finding the middle element.</p><p><strong>Correct solution:</strong> Rearrange the list to <code>3, 6, 12, 14, 18, 23, 28</code>. The middle value among <code>7</code> ordered numbers is the 4th item, which is <code>14</code>.</p><h3>Practice Problem 2: Calculating the Mean</h3><p><strong>Question:</strong> What is the mean of the data set <code>19, 12, 26, 30, 21, 30</code>?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>21</code> | <code>18</code> | <code>138</code> | <code>23</code></p><p><strong>Correct Answer:</strong> <code>23</code></p><p><strong>Wrong attempt:</strong></p><p>Stopping after calculating the total sum of <code>19 + 12 + 26 + 30 + 21 + 30 = 138</code> and selecting <code>138</code>.</p><p><strong>What is wrong:</strong> <code>138</code> is the sum of the numbers, not the mean. The sum must be divided by the number of data points, <code>n = 6</code>.</p><p><strong>Correct solution:</strong> Calculate the sum: <code>19 + 12 + 26 + 30 + 21 + 30 = 138</code>. Divide the sum by <code>6</code>: <code>138 / 6 = 23</code>.</p><h3>Practice Problem 3: Finding a Missing Value</h3><p><strong>Question:</strong> The mean of <code>7</code> numbers is <code>18</code>. <code>6</code> of the numbers are <code>25, 23, 23, 5, 5, 30</code>. What is the remaining number?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>15</code> | <code>18</code> | <code>&minus;3</code> | <code>126</code></p><p><strong>Correct Answer:</strong> <code>15</code></p><p><strong>Wrong attempt:</strong></p><p>Subtracting the sum of the <code>6</code> known numbers from the mean itself: <code>18 &minus; 111 = &minus;93</code> (or selecting <code>18</code> as an assumption).</p><p><strong>What is wrong:</strong> The mean is not the total sum of the <code>7</code> numbers. You must multiply the mean by <code>7</code> first to find the required total sum.</p><p><strong>Correct solution:</strong> Find the total sum needed for <code>7</code> numbers: <code>7 &times; 18 = 126</code>. Sum the <code>6</code> known values: <code>25 + 23 + 23 + 5 + 5 + 30 = 111</code>. Subtract the known sum from the target sum: <code>126 &minus; 111 = 15</code>.</p><h3>Practice Problem 4: Median with Repeated Values</h3><p><strong>Question:</strong> What is the median of the data set <code>4, 18, 3, 16, 14, 20, 18</code>?</p><p><strong>Choices:</strong> <code>18</code> | <code>17</code> | <code>16</code> | <code>15</code></p><p><strong>Correct Answer:</strong> <code>16</code></p><p><strong>Wrong attempt:</strong></p><p>Selecting <code>18</code> because it appears twice or sits in the second position of the unsorted list.</p><p><strong>What is wrong:</strong> Mode or unsorted order was used instead of sorting all <code>7</code> numbers in order.</p><p><strong>Correct solution:</strong> Sort all values including duplicates: <code>3, 4, 14, 16, 18, 18, 20</code>.
